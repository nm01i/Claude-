## Project overview
Giftmatch is a concept for an online gift-selection service. A short questionnaire, ready surprise boxes and a free consultation help visitors find a gift in minutes. The work covers a wireframe, a prototype, the final desktop UI and responsive layouts.

## Brief and goals
A clear, emotional and intuitive interface that helps people find a gift quickly, without a complicated search. The core idea is selection scenarios instead of a product catalogue.

## Audience
People choosing a gift for someone else, as the copy implies: a partner, a colleague, a child or a friend, with delivery across EU countries. The UI copy is in Russian.

## Design challenge
Gift buyers often do not know what they want, so a product grid fails them. The page had to guide the choice and still offer a human fallback.

## Process
The design moved through five stages in Figma, each kept as a frame:
- Wireframe with placeholder copy
- Mid-fidelity prototype with real copy
- Modular "lego" layout built from cards
- Final desktop UI on a 12-column grid
- Mobile layout and device mockups

## Key design decisions
- Selection scenarios instead of a catalogue: questionnaire, surprise boxes, interests, age, customization
- A hero that answers what, how much, where and how fast: a gift from €20, EU delivery, ready in two minutes
- Consultation as a distinct fallback: the only solid-colour card, with a free callback
- One modular card system reused for options, advantages, FAQ and reviews
- An oversized lowercase wordmark and line illustrations carry the gift feeling while the UI stays minimal

## Visual design
A lilac and purple interface with white cards, line illustrations and an oversized wordmark.
- Purples #7553C7 and #916EE4, pale lilac #E3DDF4, cool white #F7F8FC, text #3C3C3C
- RockStar for display, Inter for text
- 12-column grid: 40 px margins, 20 px gutters, 75 px columns
- 10 px card radius and fully round pill buttons

## Reflection and next steps
Giftmatch is a design concept: no built version is documented.
- Next: unify the palette and type scale, design the questionnaire screens and interaction states, and add an English version
